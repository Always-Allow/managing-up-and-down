# How I showed up

## What this skill does

It reads my transcripts and turns how I showed up into a few numbers I can actually move, this week against last week. Everyone walks out of a meeting thinking it went fine, because we remember the parts we prepared. The transcript knows how far in my main point arrived, how much of the talking was mine, and whether anybody answered the thing I asked for.

## What it reads first

Work these three out before step 1, then tell me what you found in one line so I can correct you.

1. **Whose habits this is about: always mine.** I am the person running this. If this project is about a colleague, they are who I was talking to. Never measure them, never compare us, and never report on how they showed up.
2. **What I am working on.** If this project has an About me.md with a working-on line in it, take it from there and say so. If it does not, carry on without it. Never ask me for it, never invent one, and never hold up the work because you do not have it. Only the one step below that names it uses it, and that step says what to do when it is missing.
3. **What each measure needs, checked before you promise it.** Say which of these you have, because each number needs different things and a missing one means that number cannot be produced:
   - Minutes to my point needs transcripts with timestamps and speaker labels.
   - Share of the talking needs speaker labels.
   - Follow-up questions I asked needs speaker labels.
   - Whether my 1:1s ended with a next step needs the transcript of the end of each 1:1.
   - Whether my updates got a reply needs the whole thread, not just what I sent. My sent mail
     on its own proves what I wrote and where the ask sat, and proves nothing about answers.
     It also needs a fair window: something I sent this morning has not had time to be answered.

   Name what you are missing and offer the measures you can actually do. Do not estimate a number from a meeting whose transcript you do not have, and do not substitute one measure for another without telling me.

## Steps

1. **Use this week's transcripts, with last week's as the comparison.** Say how many meetings are in each week. If there are fewer than three in either, say so and treat the comparison as weak rather than reporting a trend.

2. **For each meeting, identify my main point first,** and say which speaking turn you took it from, so I can disagree with the choice. Everything about timing depends on it.

3. **Report the measures you confirmed you can do, and no others:**
   - **Minutes to my point:** how far into the meeting my main point arrives, averaged, with the per meeting numbers shown.
   - **Share of the talking:** my words as a share of all words. Call it share of words, not share of time, because they are not the same thing.
   - **Follow-up questions I asked:** questions that build on what somebody else just said. Give the count per meeting as well as the total, because attending more meetings raises the total on its own.

4. **Add these two where the evidence for each one is actually there,** and say plainly which you skipped:
   - How many of my 1:1s ended with a clear next step. This needs the end of those transcripts, not my mail.
   - How many of my updates got a reply, and where the ask sat. This needs whole threads, and only counts
     updates old enough to have been answered. Never read my own sent copy as proof that nobody replied.

5. **Give each number this week against last,** with the calculation and the meetings behind it, so I can check any of them.

6. **End with one thing to try next week,** tied to my "I'm working on" line. One thing, not a list.

## Rules

- No filler-word counts. Transcription drops almost every um before I ever see the text, so counting them measures the tool rather than me.
- Never score my tone, my confidence, my energy or how I sounded. Measure what can be counted and leave the rest alone.
- Quote the moment behind any number I might want to argue with.
- Where an ask went unanswered, say where in the message it sat and offer moving it up as something to try. Do not tell me that will get more replies, because what the research actually compared was shorter requests against longer ones, not position.
- Never measure anyone but me, even when their words are in the same transcript.
- Leave out health or family details anyone has told me, anything my company's AI policy rules out, messages I was not part of, and passwords or logins.
- Anything about a named colleague stays in this chat. Never quote it into something I send, and never copy it into another project or into a skill's instructions.
- Do not write or send anything on my behalf.
- Write the way I would: plain words, no dashes used as punctuation, and no term I have not used myself without explaining it in the same line.
