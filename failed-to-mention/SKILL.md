# Failed to mention

## What this skill does

It looks for the places I stayed quiet: where I did not push back, where I walked into a meeting without what it needed, where I owed someone real feedback and gave them a nod instead, and where I heard something worth raising and never raised it. Then it writes what I should have said, so I can still go and say it. Nothing is ever sent, and everything it writes comes back to me as a draft.

## What it reads first

Work these three out before step 1, then tell me what you found in one line so I can correct you.

1. **Whose silence this is about: always mine.** I am the person running this. If this project is about a colleague, they are the person I owed something to, never the person being assessed. Do not look for moments where they stayed quiet, and do not report on their conduct.
2. **What I am working on.** If this project has an About me.md with a working-on line in it, take it from there and say so. If it does not, carry on without it. Never ask me for it, never invent one, and never hold up the work because you do not have it. Only the one step below that names it uses it, and that step says what to do when it is missing.
3. **What you can actually reach.** Before searching, tell me which sources you can see and the dates they cover. If you can reach none of them, say so and ask me to paste in my last month of meeting notes and messages instead of guessing.

## Steps

1. **Use the last month** unless I name a different window, and say which dates you used.

2. **Search my calendar, email, messages and meeting notes** through whatever apps you are connected to. Do not search the web.

3. **Look for these four, and label each one you find:**
   - **No pushback:** I agreed to a date, a number or a plan, and there is something in my own notes or messages showing I had a concern about it.
   - **Unprepared:** a meeting asked me for something specific and the record shows I did not bring it.
   - **Feedback owed:** I responded to someone's work with praise or with nothing, and there is something elsewhere showing I had a different view.
   - **Never raised:** the same risk reached me more than once and appears nowhere that anyone could act on it.

4. **Show the evidence for each one,** with the date and the meeting or message it came from. Where the record shows what I did but not what I thought, say so and put it to me as a question instead: "You agreed to the Q4 date on 12 September. Did you have a concern at the time?" Never assert a doubt, an opinion or an intention that the sources do not actually contain.

5. **Ask me which one to work on, and wait.** If you have a working-on line, put the moment that bears most on it first and say why. Write nothing until I answer. Then write what I should have said, in the shape that fits who it is for:
   - **For me:** a prep note for the next time it comes round, saying what to bring and what to ask for.
   - **For someone on my team:** a short message that says the real thing, kindly and specifically, with the part I skipped in it.
   - **For my leadership:** a short flag, raised early. Put a number in it only if the sources give you one, and if they do not, leave the number out and tell me which one to find.

6. **Mark every draft as a draft,** and say that sending it is mine to do.

## Rules

- Never send, schedule, post or file anything, and never set a reminder. This skill reads and drafts, and that is all it does.
- A record of agreement is not proof of a private doubt, and "looks good" is not proof of feedback withheld. Where the evidence only shows what happened, give me the question rather than the conclusion.
- This is about what I did not say, not a list of tasks I did not finish. A missed deadline is not something I failed to mention.
- Do not soften a finding that the evidence supports. A draft that says "looks good" again is no use to me.
- Leave out health or family details anyone has told me, anything my company's AI policy rules out, messages I was not part of, and passwords or logins. If one of those is the only evidence for a moment, drop the moment.
- What you found out about a named colleague stays in this chat. Never copy it into another project or into a skill's instructions. A draft addressed to that person is the exception and the whole point: it may say what I saw in their work, because that is the feedback I owed them. It may never carry anything about a third person.
- Four or five moments at most, because that is as many as I can act on.
- Write the way I would: plain words, no dashes used as punctuation, and no term I have not used myself without explaining it in the same line.
