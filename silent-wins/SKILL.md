# Silent wins

## What this skill does

It reads a stretch of my work and lists what I did that never reached anyone. Most people write a review from memory and come up with two or three things, because memory keeps the launches and drops the rest. My calendar, messages and documents kept all of it. Every line comes back with the place it was found, so I can check it before I use it.

## What it reads first

Work these three out before step 1, then tell me what you found in one line so I can correct you.

1. **Whose work this is about: always mine.** I am the person running this. If this project is about a colleague, they are who the result is for, or the person it concerns. They are never the one being assessed, and nothing here reports on their conduct.
2. **What I am working on.** If this project has an About me.md with a working-on line in it, take it from there and say so. If it does not, carry on without it. Never ask me for it, never invent one, and never hold up the work because you do not have it. Only the one step below that names it uses it, and that step says what to do when it is missing.
3. **What you can actually reach.** Before searching, tell me which sources you can see and the dates they cover. If you can reach none of them, say so and ask me to paste in my last month of calendar entries and sent updates instead of guessing.

## Steps

1. **Ask which of the three I want, and wait.** Each one searches for something different, so do not search before I answer.
   - **For a review:** what I did that proved my business impact.
   - **For my team:** what other teams have said about my team's work.
   - **For my leadership:** where my work showed up downstream, meaning the effect it had on another team after it left mine.

2. **Set the period and say the exact dates before you search.** A review means the quarter now ending. A team meeting or an update means the time since the last one. If you cannot see when the last one was, use the last month, say that is what you did, and let me correct it.

3. **Search my calendar, email, messages and meeting notes** across that period, through whatever apps you are connected to. Do not search the web.

4. **Apply the test that belongs to the direction I picked.** They are not the same test, and an item can pass one and fail another.
   - For a review: I did it, and it appears in no update, review or 1:1 note.
   - For my team: somebody outside my team said it, and my team has not been told.
   - For my leadership: my work changed something outside my own team, AND that effect has not already been said in an update or a review. Impact on its own is not enough; the whole list is things that went unsaid.

5. **Link every line:** what it was, the date, and the meeting, message or document it came from. Anything with nothing behind it does not go on the list.

6. **Sort by what it proves,** putting the items that landed outside my own team first.

7. **End with one line I could say out loud.** Of everything on the list, pick the item that best shows movement on what I am working on, and ask me for that line now if you do not have it. Write it the way I would say it in the room, and tell me to check it before I use it.

## Rules

- Only report what you found. If a source was out of reach, name it rather than quietly working around it.
- Never invent an item, a date or a number.
- Write "not found in what I could see" rather than "nobody noticed" or "it was never recognised". You cannot see what people said elsewhere, and an absence in my sources is not proof of an absence in the world.
- Leave out health or family details anyone has told me, anything my company's AI policy rules out, messages I was not part of, and passwords or logins. If one of those is the only evidence for an item, drop the item.
- Anything about a named colleague stays in this chat. Never quote it into something I send, and never copy it into another project or into a skill's instructions.
- Ten lines at most, and do not write or send anything on my behalf.
- Write the way I would: plain words, no dashes used as punctuation, and no term I have not used myself without explaining it in the same line.
