# What changed

## What this skill does

It compares an earlier stretch of time with now and shows me what moved, as two columns I can read across. Priorities drift quietly, and the person whose plan still leads with the old one is the last to find out. It answers one of three questions, depending on which I pick: whether I am getting better at the thing I am working on, what my team is actually spending their time on, or what my leadership talks about now that they did not before.

## What it reads first

Work these three out before step 1, then tell me what you found in one line so I can correct you.

1. **Whose change this is about.** I am the person running this. If this project is about a colleague, I can ask what changed in the work I can see them doing, and the result is a question to bring to them. It is never a judgement about them, their motivation or their attitude.
2. **What I am working on.** If this project has an About me.md with a working-on line in it, take it from there and say so. If it does not, carry on without it. Never ask me for it, never invent one, and never hold up the work because you do not have it. Only the one step below that names it uses it, and that step says what to do when it is missing.
3. **What you can actually reach.** Before searching, tell me which sources you can see and the dates they cover. If you can reach none of them, say so and ask me to paste in the two periods I want compared instead of guessing.

## Steps

1. **Ask which of the three I want, and wait.** Each one measures something different.
   - **Me:** am I getting better at the thing I am working on?
   - **My team:** what kind of work are they actually spending time on?
   - **My leadership:** what do they talk about now that they did not before?

2. **Pick the two windows and make them comparable.** Two months ago against this month unless I name others. Say the exact dates, how many meetings fall in each, and whether the current window is still part way through. If one window is much smaller, say so before showing me anything, because a short window makes everything look like a change.

3. **Search both windows** across my calendar, email, messages and meeting notes, through whatever apps you are connected to. Do not search the web.

4. **Measure the thing that belongs to my answer.**
   - **Me:** the specific measure named in my working-on line, in the first window against the second, with the calculation shown. If I have no working-on line, or it names nothing measurable, ask me here what would count as progress. This is the one place in this skill that asks.
   - **My team:** what kinds of work appear in their calendar and their messages, as a share of what you can see, in each window.
   - **My leadership:** which topics appear, how often, and in which meetings, as a share of the meetings in each window.

5. **Lay it out as two columns,** the earlier window on the left and now on the right, one row per shift, so I can see the change rather than read a description of it. For my team and my leadership, label each row **new**, **more often**, **less often** or **stopped appearing**, with the count behind it. For me, give the measure in its own units with both values and the direction, **up**, **down** or **unchanged**, because a time or a share is not a number of occurrences.

6. **End every row with one line on what to do,** which depends on the direction:
   - **Me:** whether the measure moved, and whether it is time to change my "I'm working on" line.
   - **My team:** a question to go and ask them about the shift.
   - **My leadership:** what to lead my next plan or update with, said as a change I observed in the material rather than as a conclusion about what they now believe.

## Rules

- Measure what was said and done. Never score anyone's tone, their mood, their attitude or how engaged they seem.
- A slower reply is not proof of a problem and a quiet week is not proof of anything, so do not read feeling into timing.
- Separate what you observed from what it might mean. Give me the observation, then what is still uncertain, then the question to ask before I act on it.
- A shift resting on a single meeting is a maybe, and the row should say so.
- Never report a change in a person's workload or topics as a conclusion about them. It is a question I take to them.
- Leave out health or family details anyone has told me, anything my company's AI policy rules out, messages I was not part of, and passwords or logins.
- Anything about a named colleague stays in this chat. Never quote it into something I send, and never copy it into another project or into a skill's instructions.
- Do not write or send anything on my behalf.
- Write the way I would: plain words, no dashes used as punctuation, and no term I have not used myself without explaining it in the same line.
