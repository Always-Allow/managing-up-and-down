# managing-up-and-down

Four skills that read your own week back to you: what you did that nobody heard about, where you stayed quiet, what has shifted around you, and how you actually land in meetings.

They come from Managing Up and Down, an Always Allow AI session held in September 2026.

Each one only ever looks at you. None of them assesses a colleague, scores anyone's tone or mood, or sends anything. They read, they show you what they found with the place they found it, and they draft. Sending stays yours.

## The four

| Skill | What it surfaces | What you get back |
|---|---|---|
| [Silent wins](silent-wins/SKILL.md) | Work you did that never made it into an update, a review or a conversation | A list of wins with dates, each linked to where it happened |
| [Failed to mention](failed-to-mention/SKILL.md) | Moments you should have said something and didn't: pushback skipped, feedback owed, a risk heard twice and never raised | A draft of what you should have said, so you can still say it |
| [What changed](what-changed/SKILL.md) | What shifted between two months, in your own habits, your team's work, or what your leadership talks about | Two columns side by side, and one line on what to do about each row |
| [How I showed up](how-i-showed-up/SKILL.md) | How you land in meetings, in your 1:1s and in your updates | Three numbers, this week against last, with the calculation behind each |

## Add it in Claude

1. Open Customize, then Skills.
2. Press the plus button, then Create skill.
3. Choose Upload a skill and pick the zip.

It installs exactly as written, in about a minute. If the Skills page is greyed out, switch on "Code execution and file creation" under Settings, then Capabilities.

## Add it in ChatGPT

ChatGPT doesn't take an upload. It makes its own version of the skill from the file.

1. Open Skills, then Create a skill.
2. Attach the zip.
3. It builds the skill from the file. This takes a few minutes, and the wording will differ a little from the original.

## Running one

Start it by name, in a new chat or inside one of your projects. Say "run Silent wins".

Three of the four just run. **What changed** asks what counts as progress when you point it at yourself, because it cannot measure improvement without knowing what you are trying to improve.

If you keep a project with an `About me.md` in it, and that file has a line saying what you're working on, every skill reads it from there and none of them asks.

## What they need to see

They are only as good as what they can reach. Each one tells you which sources it can see and the dates they cover before it searches, and says plainly when it cannot do a measurement rather than estimating one.

They leave out health or family details, anything your company's AI policy rules out, messages you weren't part of, and passwords. Anything they turn up about a named colleague stays in that chat.
